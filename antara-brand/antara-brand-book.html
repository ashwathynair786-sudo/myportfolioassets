The name

What Antara means

The name comes from Sanskrit and from Indian classical music. Both meanings point the same way: inward, and then upward.

अंतरा
antarā · /ən̪.t̪ə.ɾaː/
Within
From the Sanskrit antar: inner, inside, the space within. It is the root of antaratma, the inner self. Antara is about the light a person, a room or a home carries inside.
The rising verse
In a raga or a song, the antara is the second movement. After the sthayi settles low, the antara climbs into the upper octave. It is the moment a song lifts, and the moment a dark room changes when a lamp is lit.
The space between
Antar also means interval, the gap between two things. Light lives in that gap, between a lamp and what it touches.

Logo meaning

A lamp that reveals a name

The logo is a scene, not a symbol. A street lamp bends over the dark, and only what stands inside its light can be seen.

The shade

A half dome drawn in a single white line. It reads as a lamp, and also as half a sun at dusk. It is the only part of the logo that is outline only, because the shade is structure and holds no light.

The bulb

A small hollow cut into the beam, where the light begins. It is the antar, the inside. Everything else in the logo comes out of this point.

The beam

One cone of warm amber that fades to black as it falls, like real light. It is tilted, not centred, so the logo feels like a moment someone walked into.

The pole

A single dark vertical set to the side. It holds the light up without standing in it. That is the role Antara wants in people's homes: support, not spectacle.

The wordmark

ANTARA in Alta, widely spaced. Each letter is lit from the top and fades at its feet, so the name sits inside the light rather than on top of it.

The tagline

"Light from within" in a warm cream serif. The italic f gives a small flicker in an otherwise calm line.

Switch the lamp off and the name disappears. Antara only exists in the light, and that idea runs through everything else in this book.

Brand philosophy

Every room has an antara, a quiet inner light. We make the lamps that let it show.

Warmth before brightness

We choose amber over white and glow over glare. A good lamp makes a room feel lived in, not lit up.

The line holds, the light speaks

Objects are drawn as outlines. Colour belongs only to light. This keeps the brand calm and makes every glow count.

Darkness is part of the design

We leave space for night. Dark grounds and generous margins are what let a small light feel big.

From diya to street lamp

Antara holds the oil lamp at a threshold and the street lamp outside the window. Heritage and the everyday sit in one family.

Logo usage

Versions of the logo

The full lamp scene is the primary logo. Where there is no room for it, use the wordmark lockup or the lamp mark. The lit gradient is only used on dark grounds.

Clear space

Keep empty space around every version equal to the height of the A in the wordmark. Nothing else enters the light.

Minimum size

Wordmark: 25 mm or 120 px wide. Drop the tagline below 40 mm or 200 px. Lamp mark: 16 px.

Don't

  • Light the logo in cool white or blue.
  • Put the lit gradient on a light background.
  • Move the beam so it comes from below or the left.
  • Fill the shade or outline the beam.

Typography

Three typefaces, three jobs

Alta is kept for the logo. Cormorant Garamond is the voice of headlines and quotes. Montserrat does the quiet work of body text, labels and interfaces. All three come from the source file.

Logo only

Alta

A tall, open display face with long, light strokes. Use it only for the wordmark and never set new words in it, so the name stays unique.

Illustration

A family of lamps

The illustrations show many kinds of lamp, from the diya to the street lamp. Each one is drawn as a border only. The only filled shapes are flame, bulb and beam, so the light is always the subject.

Line

A single 1.5 px stroke in Ash on dark, or Soot on paper. Round joins, no fills on objects.

Light

Filament fading to transparent. Cores of flames and bulbs go to Glow and near white.

Direction

Light falls down and outward. Beams are cones, flames are teardrops, halos are soft circles.

Motion

Lamps flicker on and switch off. They never fade slowly, because real lamps don't.

Tap any lamp to switch it off or on.

Applications

The brand at work

The lamps travel across every touchpoint. Each piece uses one lamp, one light, and lots of dark or paper around it.

Voice

How Antara speaks

Like the hour after sunset: warm, unhurried and close. Short sentences. Concrete words about rooms, evenings and people, not watts and features.
