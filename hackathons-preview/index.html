The brief

Context

HackerEarth runs hackathons for companies that want to find developers, test new ideas, or build a community around their platform. Each event needs its own campaign, launched across the web, social, email and on the ground.

Challenge

Every client arrives with its own brand guidelines and its own audience. The campaign has to feel like the client's event, still read as a HackerEarth hackathon, and be ready to ship in a few weeks.

Solution

I built a repeatable hackathon kit: one key visual per event, then extended into a microsite, a social series, an email sequence and print for the venue. The structure stays the same from event to event, so each new client gets a campaign that looks bespoke and still ships on schedule.

    The brief

    Booking Holdings wanted to hire its next intern class through a hackathon. The campaign had to reach final-year engineering students, many of whom had never entered a hackathon, and make a six-month internship feel worth competing for.

    What I made

    An event identity for HackCellence: The Embark Edition. A friendly, all-blue illustration of students building together sets the tone, and "Think. Build. Ship." carries the message. The microsite turns a long list of rounds, dates, rules and eligibility checks into short, scannable sections, and a LinkedIn series and a set of emailers took the campaign to students and to college placement teams.

    The brief

    Airwallex came with an established brand for the hackathon. My job was to turn it into a campaign: a microsite and a set of emailers that feel fully Airwallex and still work as a HackerEarth event.

    What I made

    I worked inside the ABH system, using its pixel logo, mono type and orange palette. The microsite lays out a lot of detail, from twelve track starter kits to the timeline, prizes, partners and FAQs, in a dark, grid-based layout that stays easy to scan. The social kit takes one creative across Facebook, Instagram stories and LinkedIn.
